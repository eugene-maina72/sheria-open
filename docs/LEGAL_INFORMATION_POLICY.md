# Legal information policy

## Purpose

SheriaOpen provides legal information and civic education. It does not provide individual legal advice or representation.

## Standard answer structure

A public answer should contain:

1. **Legal status** - for example, current law, proposed Bill, repealed or uncertain.
2. **Plain-language answer** - a concise explanation based on retrieved evidence.
3. **Practical meaning** - general implications without predicting an individual's outcome.
4. **Important exceptions** - limitations, conditions and unresolved questions.
5. **Official sources** - exact document and provision citations.
6. **Last verified** - the date of the status and source check.
7. **Escalation** - when professional or emergency help may be appropriate.

## Required refusals or limitations

The system should refuse to invent an answer when evidence is missing. It should avoid personalised conclusions where material facts, procedural history or professional judgement are required.

High-risk topics include:

- active criminal allegations;
- arrest and detention;
- court or statutory deadlines;
- domestic violence and child protection;
- eviction and imminent loss of housing;
- immigration and citizenship;
- land-title disputes;
- tax enforcement;
- election offences;
- professional-discipline proceedings; and
- any situation involving immediate danger.

The platform may provide general information and source links while clearly recommending professional assistance.

## Political neutrality

The platform may explain electoral law, public participation and the legal effects of Bills. It must not rank candidates, recommend parties, create political persuasion profiles or tailor legal explanations to manipulate voting behaviour.

## Correction standard

A reported legal error should be reproducible, reviewed and corrected with an audit trail. Serious errors should trigger review of related cached answers and evaluation cases.
